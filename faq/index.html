FAQ

Good questions.

Straight answers about thermal search, the rules in Indiana, the weather, and what it costs.

01Is it legal to use a drone to find a deer in Indiana?

Yes, to recover one. Indiana law (IC 14-22-6-16) lets a person fly a drone with infrared to locate and recover a legally taken animal. What’s not allowed is using a drone to scout, locate or detect game as an aid to taking it, from 14 days before the season through the end of legal hunting hours on its last day. The law also doesn’t let anyone enter or recover on someone else’s land without the landowner’s permission. Rules change, so check the current Indiana DNR regulations each season.

02How soon should I call?

As soon as you know you might need help, even if you plan to wait before searching. We can talk through timing on the phone. Thermal works on temperature difference, so a deer is easiest to pick out while it is still warmer than its surroundings, and that contrast fades over time.

03Do I need landowner permission?

Yes. We need permission to recover a deer on any property, and the hunter does too. If the deer may have crossed onto a neighbor’s ground, ask them before we arrive. It saves a lot of time.

04Can thermal see through the trees?

Not through solid cover. Leafless hardwoods let a lot of heat through the gaps, which is why late season and night searches work well. Dense evergreens, cedar thickets and full summer canopy can hide an animal completely.

05Can you find my cat or dog?

Often, if they are out in the open: fields, fencerows, woods edges and creek banks. Thermal can’t see through roofs, barns, culverts or thick brush, so a pet hiding under something may be out of view. We’ll tell you honestly what we think the odds are when you call.

06Will the drone scare my pet?

We fly high and keep the drone quiet over the animal. Once we find them, we guide you in on foot and you do the approach.

07Is this a legal land survey?

No. “Land surveying” here means aerial views, headcounts and checks of your ground. For legal property boundaries, use a licensed land surveyor.

08Can you count cattle at night?

Yes. Thermal headcounts work best in cool air, early in the morning or after dark, when animals show up clearly against the ground.

09What does “Part 107 certified” mean?

Part 107 is the FAA’s rulebook for commercial drone flights. A certified remote pilot has passed the FAA knowledge test and keeps current with recurrent training. Flying at night also requires that training plus anti-collision lighting on the drone.

10What weather can you fly in?

Not in rain, heavy fog or strong wind. Cool, still, dry nights give the best thermal picture. If the weather is against a good search, we’ll tell you straight.

11What drone do you use?

A DJI Matrice 4T, an enterprise drone built for search work. It carries a 640 × 512 thermal camera, zoom cameras, a laser rangefinder and a near-infrared auxiliary light. See the gear page for more.

12How much does a search cost?

Deer Recovery is $250 for up to 2 hours, plus $100 per hour for each additional hour, a $50 finder’s fee (dead or alive), and a $1 per mile travel fee after 50 miles from Rockville. Lost Pet Recovery is $150 per hour, with the same $1 per mile travel fee after 50 miles from Rockville. Herd & Land Surveying depends on the job; call for more information.

13What areas do you cover?

Parke County and the surrounding counties: Vermillion, Fountain, Montgomery, Putnam, Clay and Vigo. Further out? Call and ask.
